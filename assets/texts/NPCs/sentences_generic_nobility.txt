• My grandfather rode to war with fifty lances. I ride to my moneylender with fifty excuses.
• Trade? Heavens, no. I merely own the land, the mill, the toll bridge and the market rights. The trading is done by little people.
• A peasant who takes a deer from my forest hangs. A merchant who takes the whole forest is merely collecting on a debt.
• I heard the Bishop's hat is so tall he has to duck to enter the Cathedral.
• Those new cannon make a castle wall about as useful as a lace curtain. Father spent a fortune on our walls.
• My tailor says Burgundian fashion is finished and everything must now be slashed and puffed. I paid good silver to have my sleeves ruined.
• They say the Earth is a sphere, but I reckon they've just had too much cider.
• For a modest fee my genealogist traced my line back to Charlemagne. For a slightly larger fee, back to Adam.
• A merchant asked for my daughter's hand yesterday. I said no. Then I saw his ledger and said I'd think about it.
• I would like to propose my favorite book to you but I hardly doubt that you can read.
• I heard that trading licenses are cheaper when bought directly with the guilds.
• The Emperor has asked me for money again. I have asked my tenants. My tenants have asked God. Only God has not replied.
• A falcon costs more than a farm and has twice my wife's temper. Hawking truly is the sport of kings.
• A gentleman never carries anything himself. That is what servants, pack horses and younger sons are for.
• Jousting is terribly dangerous. At the last tourney I lost a tooth, a horse and my cousin's wager, in that order.
• I read Latin, French and a little Greek. I just can't read my steward's accounts, which I suspect is the point.
