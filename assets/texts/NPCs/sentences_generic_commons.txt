• Don't like those windmills, they make lots of noise and look like monstrosities.
• Bless you! But keep your sneezes to yourself next time, or the pox follow after.
• Newfangled muskets! Whatever happened to a good, honest bow?
• The mud in this town is thicker than the Mayor’s skull.
• The tithe man took every tenth sheaf of my barley. If he wants every tenth sermon back, he can have those for free.
• The lord's mill keeps a sixteenth of my flour for the grinding. I ground at home once. The bailiff's fine cost me more than the mill ever took.
• Mind your purse; the cutpurses here are faster than a frightened rabbit.
• The bathhouse shut when the French pox came over the Alps. Now I smell like an honest Christian again.
• My boots have more holes than a cheese from the lowlands.
• Seven years my cousin's been apprenticed to the smith, and last week they finally let him hold the hammer.
• Everyone sells grain after the harvest and everyone needs it before the next one. Buy in autumn, sell in spring, and pray the mice don't learn the trick.
• The rats in the cellar are getting so big they’ve started charging me rent.
• Wednesday fish, Friday fish, Ember Day fish, Lenten fish. By Easter I'll have grown gills.
• Curfew bell rings, fires out, doors barred. Only the night watchman may wander the streets, and he's usually the drunkest man in town.
• A printer in Mainz makes a hundred Bibles faster than a monk copies one. I still can't read any of them, mind.
• The Town Crier has a voice like a rusted gate on a windy night.
• Lost my hat in the river, my cow to the murrain and my wife to the tanner. The hat I truly miss.
• Jump the Midsummer fire and you'll have a good harvest, they say. Jump it badly and you'll have no eyebrows.
• I heard that trading licenses are cheaper when bought directly with the guilds.
• The pardoner sold me a feather from the Archangel Gabriel. Funny, it looks just like the one my goose lost.
• The barber pulled my tooth, bled my arm and trimmed my beard, all for one penny. Only the beard came out well.
• Slaughter the pig at Martinmas and salt it well, and you'll eat all winter. Forget the salt and by Candlemas you'll be eating the rope you hung it with.
• The almanac says Tuesday is unlucky for sowing, Wednesday for journeys and Friday for everything. I've had a very restful week.
• When the bell rings for a fire, everyone runs with a bucket. When it rings for mass, everyone suddenly has a bad leg.
• The forest is the lord's, the deer are the lord's, even the acorns my pigs eat are the lord's. Only the fleas are mine.
• The council forbids a man of my station to wear silver buttons. No great hardship: I own no silver and two buttons.
• Beeswax for the church, tallow for the rich, and a rush dipped in mutton fat for me. It smells like supper and gives half the light.
• When grain goes up the loaf keeps its price and shrinks instead. The baker calls that the assize. I call it a conjuring trick.
• Three days' harvest work I owe the lord, and he feeds us while we do it. Weak ale and hard cheese, so I pay for the privilege of being fed.
• My daughter spins from first light to last, and it takes six spinners to keep one weaver in thread. Nobody sings a ballad about that.
• The gate shuts at dusk. Come late and you sleep outside with the pigs, unless the gatekeeper likes the sound of a coin.
• Firewood costs more every winter. One day we'll burn the furniture and sit on the floor like the desert saints.
• The guilds play the Bible at Corpus Christi. The shipwrights get Noah's flood, the bakers get the Last Supper, and the devil always has the best lines.
• The clean well is three streets away, so we drink small beer instead. Safer than the river, and better company.
